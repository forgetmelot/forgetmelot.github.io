On rarity ratings

Who doesn't want shiny Pokémon? Who doesn't want to say "I have found a rare bird"? But I won't do rarity justice if I just slapped on a few stars and called it a day.

Rarity is not a unitary construct. I have seen certain species in different parts of the world, and they are certainly not equally abundant in these areas. Some birds are locally abundant but globally less so. Others are not abundant throughout their extensive range. Vagrants that attract hundreds of twitchers, might be garden birds elsewhere. The sampling context also matters: it would be much harder to find a skulky species by mere observation than say, mist net trapping. Many species are also more easily observed at particular times of the year and times of the day.

It may be best to refrain from describing a species as rare. Instead, rarity is relevant to events, i.e. it is rare to observe this bird at this place at this time, which isn't as exciting, or as easy to capture with a linear scale.

On photography

I don't exactly consider myself a "bird photographer". I feel like all I need is a sharp, ID-able shot with as much of the bird as possible, and anything more is a bonus. It bores me to spend hours at a single spot to get the perfect shot, or simply waiting for the target bird to appear. Alas, I do realise that is the only way to see certain species, so I begrudgingly show up and stand. Nonetheless, I have been learning camera techniques, though obviously with much less enthusiasm than studying a field guide.

My favourite birds

I am quite particular about colour. I dislike overly saturated colours, and lean more towards subtler tones. For instance, instead of the bright red of a Scarlet Tanager #F0190A, I prefer the more muted, variegated red of a male Crossbill #CF584A. Green broadbills #0DF705 are certainly cool, but I can't stare at them for more than a few minutes. And yet my heart flutters every now and then for the green rump of a Chaffinch #AABA43. As such, my favourite birds are often coloured to blend in; some could even be described as cryptically plumaged.

My favourite birds come in two major categories. The first are extremely rotund species, either long-tailed or almost tailless. This includes things like Long-tailed Tit, Dartford Warbler, Vinous-throated Parrotbill, cupwings, and fairywrens. The second group of birds have bizarre appearances and behaviours, but if you think about it seriously it kind of makes sense. This includes things like bittern, nightjar, grasshopper warbler, and woodcock. And of course, the are overlaps between these two categories, and that makes my favourites of favourites. Bearded Reedling is a strong contender with its tiny beady eyes and max fluff in winter. And then there's Red Grouse, which I also have a knack for mimicking the calls of. I'm still deciding which HK birds I like the most, since most of them are not particularly round due to the hot weather...
